“Tienes que leer entre las lineas.''  My Guatemalan mother always told me to read between the lines. Her first language isn’t English, so while I grew up, I had to learn how to piece together her words and find their meaning. One day my mother was recounting a story to my dad and me; she said, “I took the rabbit and -” then, there was a series of hand movements depicting a bloody battle. Instantly, I knew that she meant that she cut herself with the corkscrew opener that looked like a bunny. My dad looked at me in awe, like I had uncovered ancient treasures. 
However, my mother’s impact isn’t solely within her words; she has provided me with experiences that have influenced how I want to touch the world. For example, every Sunday at 6:30 am, I would be awakened by the sound of Juan Gabriel. If you were to peer into the kitchen, you would see my mother adorned with a hairnet and an apron with the words “Anyone can cook.” She would be singing completely off-pitch while starting her first batch of El Salvadorean quesadillas - a cheesy pound cake.  At first, the sound of the music made me furrow myself deeper into my pillow. Until one day, my mother barged into my room - without knocking, of course - and asked me how to use Google Sheets. She wanted to expand her baking hobby into a small business. So, after hours of back and forth between two languages - and the occasional sign language my mother concocted - I, a groggy teenager, and my mother generated a plan. My mom would perfect her recipes for her delicious baked goods and I, how my mom would say it, “Do the computer,” paired perfectly with her hands mimicking typing on a keyboard. 
First, I started with the Google Sheet. I watched tutorial videos on the uses and functions of a sheet, and for every “aha” moment, I would turn around and say, “Mama, look, you can do __!” She would look up from her intense Candy Crush game and respond with an unmoved “wow.” After a few days, I mastered the basics and created a form where people could order my mother’s goods which transferred onto my now color-coded sheet. This gave me a taste of how I could contribute to my mother’s dream. I started going out with my mother to deliver the food, and as my Tia Betty said, I began to look forward to Sundays. 
Week after week, I habitually began to wake up with my mom, and her business started to grow as people became seized by the deliciousness of my mother’s food. And if you peeked into the kitchen, you would no longer see just one woman garnished with a hairnet and fashionable apron singing off-pitch to Juan Gabriel, but two.
